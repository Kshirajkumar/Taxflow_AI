What we build

A workspace for UAE accounting firms and tax agents. Clients send bills on WhatsApp. Taxflow files them, reads the tax invoice fields in English and Arabic, chases what is missing, tracks every FTA deadline per client, and produces VAT 201 and corporate tax worksheets with invoice evidence behind each number.

What we do not build

  • Not a full accounting ledger. We export to Zoho, QuickBooks, Xero and Tally.
  • Not an FTA-accredited e-invoicing provider. We partner with one.
  • Not a filing agent. The licensed firm files on EmaraTax.

Who pays

Boutique accounting firms, corporate tax consultancies, registered tax agents and freelance accountants serving SMEs. We charge the firm, not the end client.

How we make money

Monthly subscription per firm: AED 249, AED 799 or AED 1,999 by client volume, plus fair usage on documents and WhatsApp messages. Later add-ons: client portal, e-invoicing referral share, network plans.

🎯Recommended strategy: win 10 design-partner firms by December 2026, ship the core loop (collect, read, track, prepare) before the 1 January 2027 e-invoicing go-live, then launch paid plans in Q1 2027 with 40+ paying firms by September 2027.

2. Why UAE, why now

Five forces make the next 12 months unusually favourable.

1. Corporate tax created bookkeeping demand

More than 640,000 businesses are registered for corporate tax, and the first filing round for December 2024 year-ends completed by end of September 2025. Almost every licensed business, including free zone companies, must register. Books must now be IFRS-based and kept for years (VAT records 5 years, corporate tax records 7 years).

2. E-invoicing forces structured data

Invoices must be structured XML in the PINT AE format, sent through a Ministry of Finance accredited provider. A PDF emailed to a client is not an e-invoice under this system. The FTA receives tax data through the five-corner Peppol model. B2C is excluded for now.

3. WhatsApp is the default business channel

Reported WhatsApp penetration is above 80–90% of UAE smartphone users. Clients already send photos of bills to their accountant on WhatsApp. Today that is unmanaged chat history.

4. Accountants are price-squeezed and busy

Outsourced accounting is commonly quoted between AED 500 and AED 5,000 a month per client, with many SMEs at AED 1,500–5,000. Firms win by handling more clients with the same staff, so tools that remove document chasing pay for themselves quickly.

5. The software gap is the workflow, not the ledger

Zoho Books, Wafeq, QuickBooks, Xero and Tally all cover UAE VAT. What accountants still do by hand is collect documents, reconcile evidence and track deadlines across dozens of clients.

UAE accountants lose hours every week chasing, sorting and retyping client documents that arrive as photos in WhatsApp chats. Corporate tax and e-invoicing raise the stakes: more filings, stricter evidence and structured data. Key user stories and acceptance criteria (MVP)

Collect documents

As a firm owner, I want to send a document request to 50 clients in one action so that I stop messaging them one by one.

  • Select clients, template, language and send time.
  • Only clients with recorded consent receive messages.
  • No message goes out between 8 pm and 8 am, or on weekends, unless the owner overrides.
  • Delivery and read status visible per client.

Capture incoming files

As an accountant, I want every photo a client sends to land in the right folder so that nothing stays buried in chat.

  • Attachment saved within 30 seconds to the client’s Raw folder for the correct month.
  • Checklist item ticks when a matching document type arrives.
  • Unmatched files go to an “Unsorted” list for a one-click assignment.

Read invoices

As a bookkeeper, I want data extracted automatically so that I review instead of type.

  • Target for printed English tax invoices: at least 95% of key fields correct without edit (measured on design-partner data).
  • Arabic and bilingual invoices supported, with a lower first-release target that we publish honestly.
  • Fields below the confidence limit are highlighted and must be confirmed.

Track deadlines

As an owner, I want to see who is behind so that I act before a late filing.

  • Each client shows a progress bar built from its task steps.
  • Clients with a filing due within 7 days and less than 60% progress show as at risk.
  • Dates are editable and every change is logged.

Prepare VAT 201

As an accountant, I want box values with evidence so that I can file quickly and defend the numbers.

  • Standard-rated sales appear by emirate, with zero-rated, exempt, imports and reverse charge in their own lines.
  • Every figure opens the list of invoices behind it.
  • Draft only. The system never submits to the FTA.

Stay in control

As an owner, I want approvals and logs so that I stay professionally responsible.

  • Approval mode is on by default for outgoing messages.
  • Every edit records who, when, before and after.
  • Export the audit log as a file.

Explicit non-goals for v1

  • Posting journal entries or replacing the accounting system.
  • Becoming an accredited e-invoicing provider.
  • Submitting to the FTA on the firm’s behalf.
  • Selling directly to end-client SMEs.
  • Marketing broadcasts. We use utility messages tied to the firm’s existing service.

7. Architecture and technology choices

Keep the first version simple. Buy commodity parts, build the workflow.

System components

  • Web app for firms, with Arabic and English.
  • WhatsApp service through a BSP’s API, with webhooks for inbound media and delivery status.
  • Ingestion pipeline that validates files, removes duplicates and queues them.
  • Extraction service that combines OCR with a language model and rule checks (TRN format, arithmetic, VAT rate).
  • Rules engine for UAE deadlines, VAT categories and worksheet mapping.
  • Vault in UAE-region object storage with per-firm encryption keys.
  • Export and API layer for accounting systems.
  • Audit and billing services.

Hosting and AI processing

  • Host in a UAE region from a major cloud with UAE data centres.
  • Personal data may leave the UAE only to countries or under safeguards allowed by the PDPL. Sending client documents to a foreign AI service can be a cross-border transfer.
  • Prefer model processing inside the UAE. If you use an outside provider, use contractual safeguards, minimise what is sent and get legal review first.
  • Measure cost and accuracy per document type and route easy documents to cheaper paths.

Buy vs build

  • Buy: WhatsApp BSP, cloud, OCR baseline, payments, email, monitoring.
  • Build: UAE rules engine, review queue, deadline engine, worksheets, firm workflow.
  • Partner: e-invoicing provider. Becoming one needs Peppol certification and verifiable operating experience, which a new company will not have.

WhatsApp cost reality

Meta now bills per delivered template message. Reported UAE rates are roughly AED 0.04–0.06 for utility and about AED 0.16–0.18 for marketing. Replies inside a customer’s 24-hour window are free, and Meta has billed in AED since April 2026. A firm with 100 clients sending 8 utility messages each a month spends around AED 40. That is small, so the real cost risk is marketing-style messaging, which we avoid.

13. Legal and compliance checklist

Company

  • Choose mainland or a free zone for the operating company. Confirm licence activity for software.
  • Register for VAT once taxable supplies exceed the threshold (AED 375,000) and charge 5% on subscriptions to UAE customers, subject to advice.
  • Register for corporate tax within the required period.
  • Bank account and a UAE payment gateway for AED billing.

WhatsApp

  • Meta business verification needs a UAE trade licence and authorised signatory details.
  • Approved templates in Arabic and English. Approval is usually within a day.
  • Documented opt-in for each client contact. Honour opt-outs.

Data protection

  • Data processing agreement with every firm.
  • Record of processing activities and a breach procedure.
  • Check whether a Data Protection Officer is needed for your scale.
  • Note that the PDPL executive regulations have been reported inconsistently. Ask a UAE data protection lawyer for the current status.

Professional boundaries

  • Taxflow prepares drafts. The licensed firm reviews and files.
  • Only FTA registered tax agents can represent taxpayers before the FTA, so market to them accurately.
  • Terms of service should make the firm responsible for its professional judgement.

E-invoicing

  • Do not claim to be an accredited provider.
  • Sign a partner agreement with an accredited provider that appears on the Ministry of Finance list.
  • Keep any invoice data routed to the partner inside the UAE.

Security

  • Independent penetration test before paid launch.
  • ISO 27001 or SOC 2 readiness plan.
  • Incident response and customer notification policy.
⚠️This is a planning document, not legal or tax advice. Have a UAE lawyer and a registered tax agent review the items above before launch.

C. Discovery interview script (20 minutes)

  1. How many clients do you handle each month, and how many are VAT-registered?
  2. Walk me through last month’s VAT for one client. Where did the documents come from?
  3. How many hours a week does your team spend chasing or retyping documents?
  4. What tools do you use, and what do you pay for them?
  5. What happens when a client misses a deadline?
  6. How ready are your clients for e-invoicing, and who will guide them?
  7. Would you use a WhatsApp-based collection tool? What would worry you?
  8. What price feels fair for 50, 100 and 200 clients?
