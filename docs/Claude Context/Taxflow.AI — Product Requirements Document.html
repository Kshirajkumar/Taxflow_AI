Product Requirements Document · v1.0

Taxflow.AI — the document and deadline hub for accounting firms

Taxflow.AI turns the messiest part of an accountant's week — chasing clients for documents, retyping bills, tracking who owes what — into an automated, auditable workflow that runs where the client already is: WhatsApp.

Owner: Product20 September 2026Market: international CA and accounting firmsStatus: pre-build, seeking design partners

1. One-page overview

If you read nothing else, read this section.

What it is

A workspace that sits on top of an accounting firm's existing tools. Clients send documents on WhatsApp. Taxflow reads them, checks them, tracks every deadline per client, and prepares filing-ready worksheets and export files. The firm still files, using its own judgement and its existing ledger software.

Who it's for

Small and mid-size accounting, bookkeeping and tax firms (2–50 staff) serving SME clients internationally. The firm is the customer. The client is a user but never pays us directly.

The core loop

Collect→Read→Check→Track→Prepare→Export

Every feature in this document exists to make one of these six steps faster, cheaper or more accurate.

Why now

Governments everywhere are digitising tax filing (structured e-invoicing, quarterly digital reporting, JSON and XML returns), which raises the amount of clean, structured data a firm must produce. At the same time, clients overwhelmingly prefer sending a photo on WhatsApp to logging into a portal. That gap between "how clients behave" and "what regulators now require" is the product opportunity.

🎯North star: the number of client documents collected, read and approved per firm per month, without the accountant manually retyping anything.

2. The problem, in detail

Five compounding problems, not one.

1. Document chasing is unpaid, invisible labour

An accountant doesn't bill for the hour spent messaging twelve clients asking for August invoices. It's overhead that scales with client count, so it caps how many clients a small firm can serve.

2. Manual data entry is slow and error-prone

Retyping a bill into a ledger takes minutes and introduces typos. Studies commonly cite manual invoice processing costing several times more than automated processing.

3. Deadlines live in someone's head

Multiplied across dozens of clients and several filing types (VAT/GST, income tax, payroll, company filings), tracking is usually a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group and a diary — three sources of truth that disagree.

4. Evidence is scattered

When a number in a tax return is questioned, the accountant needs to find the source document fast. Today that means searching email, WhatsApp and shared drives.

5. Regulators are raising the bar

Structured e-invoicing, quarterly digital reporting and JSON/XML filing formats mean "a PDF in an email" is no longer good enough in a growing number of countries. Firms that don't adapt will spend more time on compliance mechanics, not less.

None of these problems are solved by buying a better ledger. Zoho Books, QuickBooks, Xero and Tally all record transactions well. What they don't do is get the client to send the invoice in the first place, read it without a human typing, or tell the firm which of 80 clients is falling behind this week. That gap is Taxflow's entire reason to exist.

3. Vision and goals

Vision (3 years)

Every accounting firm's client relationship runs through one calm, WhatsApp-native workspace: the firm never chases a document twice, never misses a deadline, and never retypes a number that a machine could read correctly the first time.

Mission (this PRD)

Ship a product that removes at least half the time a firm spends collecting and entering client documents, without ever filing anything the firm hasn't approved.

5. Product design principles

1. A human always approves

Taxflow never sends a message, files a return, or posts a number without a person choosing to. Automation prepares; people decide. This is the single most important trust rule in the product.

2. Meet the client where they are

We don't ask SME clients to install anything or learn a portal. If they can send a photo on WhatsApp, they can use Taxflow.

3. Every number has a source

Any figure the product produces links back to the original document. If we can't show where a number came from, we don't show the number.

4. Show confidence, don't hide uncertainty

Extraction is probabilistic. The UI always shows how sure the system is, and routes anything uncertain to a person before it's used.

5. Sit on top, don't replace

Firms have already chosen a ledger. We integrate and export; we don't ask them to migrate.

6. Built for the firm, not the client

Every screen answers a firm's question: "who's behind, what needs review, what's due." The client-facing surface (WhatsApp, optional portal) is deliberately minimal.

7. Features, explained in detail

Every feature below follows the same structure: the pain point it solves, how it actually works, and why it's designed that way. Priority: P0 ships in v1, P1 follows within two quarters, P2 is later.

1Client and firm workspace setupP0

Pain point

Firms manage client lists in scattered spreadsheets, with no single record of each client's filing obligations, contact details or consent status.

Solution

One structured client record per client: legal name, tax IDs, jurisdiction, filing obligations and frequencies, primary contact, WhatsApp number, language and consent status. Bulk import from Excel/CSV.

How it works

On sign-up, the firm imports its client list. A mapping wizard matches spreadsheet columns to fields. Filing obligations can be set per client or inferred from a jurisdiction template (for example "GST-registered, monthly" or "VAT-registered, quarterly").

2WhatsApp document collection hubP0

Pain point

Clients already send bills on WhatsApp, but it's unmanaged: buried in chat history, not filed anywhere, and the accountant has to remember to ask.

Solution

A firm connects one WhatsApp Business number. All client conversations live in one inbox, with a checklist per client showing exactly what's outstanding for the current period.

How it works

Built on the official WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) via a licensed provider. Outbound requests use pre-approved message templates (required for messages sent outside a live conversation). Every inbound attachment is captured through a webhook and routed into the client's folder automatically. Consent is recorded per contact before any message is sent, and an opt-out is honoured immediately.

Why WhatsApp specifically

It's already the channel clients use informally. Formalising it — instead of asking clients to adopt a new portal — is the single biggest adoption unlock in the product.

3Smart document requests and remindersP0

Pain point

Sending the same "please send your invoices" message to 80 clients, one at a time, is exactly the kind of repetitive task that eats an accountant's week.

Solution

Bulk, templated, scheduled requests that only ask for what's actually still missing for each client, in their preferred language, with a one-tap "approve and send" step.

How it works

A request is built from a template plus per-client variables (name, period, due date, specific missing items). The firm can send immediately, schedule it, or set a recurring rule (e.g. "3 days before each period's due date"). Quiet hours prevent messages outside acceptable times. A dry-run preview shows exactly what each client will receive before it sends.

4Automatic document extraction (OCR + AI reading)P0

Pain point

Retyping every invoice, receipt and bank line is slow, boring and where most manual errors creep in.

Solution

Every incoming bill, invoice or receipt is read automatically: date, document number, party name, tax ID, line items, tax rate, and totals — with a confidence score on each field.

How it works

A combination of OCR and a document-understanding model extracts structured fields, cross-checked with rule-based validation (does the tax add up, is the tax ID the right format, does the total match the line items). Printed documents in a clear layout get the highest accuracy; handwritten or low-quality photos get flagged for closer review rather than guessed at silently.

What it deliberately does not do

It never silently "corrects" a number without flagging it, and it never claims certainty it doesn't have. Publishing honest accuracy numbers, by document type and by language, is part of the feature — not a caveat bolted on afterward.

5Review queueP0

Pain point

Fully automated data entry that nobody checks is how mistakes end up in a filed tax return. Fully manual entry is slow. Firms need a middle path.

Solution

A side-by-side view: the original document on one side, the extracted fields on the other. Low-confidence fields are visibly highlighted. One click approves; editing a field re-checks it and clears the flag.

How it works

Documents queue by status: needs review, extracted, approved. The queue also flags duplicate invoice numbers from the same supplier and tax-ID format errors, so the accountant isn't just proofreading OCR — they're catching real compliance problems before filing.

6Client document vaultP0

Pain point

"Where's the original invoice for this number?" is a common, time-consuming question during a filing or an audit, and the answer today is scattered across email, WhatsApp and shared drives.

Solution

Every client has an automatically organised folder tree: client → year → month → Raw / Extracted / Generated. Every extracted number links straight back to its source file.

How it works

Files are routed automatically as they're captured or generated — no manual filing. Search works across file names, extracted party names and amounts. Retention rules can be set per jurisdiction's record-keeping requirements.

7Deadline and workflow trackingP0

Pain point

A firm with 80 clients and five filing types per client is tracking hundreds of deadlines. Today that lives in someone's head, a spreadsheet, and a diary that all disagree.

Solution

Every client's filings appear as cards with a progress bar built from real, checkable steps (not a guess), a due date, and a clear at-risk flag when a deadline is close and progress is low.

How it works

Each filing type has a defined step sequence (for example: collect documents → extract → reconcile → prepare → client approval → file). Steps tick automatically where the system can tell (documents approved) and manually where a person must confirm (client approved the draft). The firm-wide dashboard rolls every client up into a single "who's behind" view.

8Filing worksheets and generated filesP0

Pain point

Once documents are collected, someone still has to sum them correctly into the exact boxes, schedules or files a tax authority expects — a mechanical but detail-critical step.

Solution

Worksheets and export files built directly from approved, source-linked data: for portals with no upload (type the figures in), a clean box-by-box worksheet with an invoice-level backup list; for portals that accept a file, a validated export in the required format.

How it works

A rules engine, specific to each jurisdiction and filing type, maps approved documents to the destination structure. All arithmetic is done by fixed, auditable rules — never left to a language model to "guess" a total. The output previews before it's used, and every figure remains clickable back to its source invoices.

Why this is separate from extraction

Extraction is inherently uncertain (it's reading a photo). Calculation should never be uncertain. Keeping them as separate steps, with separate trust levels, is a deliberate architecture decision (see section 8).

9Ledger exports and integrationsP0

Pain point

Firms have already chosen and trained staff on a ledger (Tally, Zoho Books, QuickBooks, Xero, Sage). Asking them to switch is a non-starter.

Solution

Approved, extracted data exports straight into the formats those systems import, or pushes directly via API where one exists, so double entry disappears.

How it works

A mapping layer converts Taxflow's internal document model into each target format (Excel/CSV for manual import, native XML/API for direct push where supported). New export targets are added as a config, not a rebuild.

10Roles, approvals and audit logP0

Pain point

A firm is professionally and legally responsible for what it files. Handing that responsibility to an unreviewable automated system is not something any serious firm will accept — nor should they.

Solution

Role-based access (owner, senior, junior, read-only), an approval step in front of every outbound message and every "generate filing" action, and an immutable log of who did what, when, and what changed.

How it works

Every state-changing action (edit a field, approve a document, send a message, generate a worksheet) writes an audit event with before/after values. The log is exportable, so it can be handed to a regulator or an internal reviewer on request.

11Firm-wide dashboardP0

Pain point

A firm owner has no single view of practice health — they find out a filing is late when the client calls, angry.

Solution

One screen: filings due this week, clients missing documents, the extraction queue, and recent automated activity — the same view every morning.

How it works

Aggregates data already produced by the other features (nothing new is computed here) into ranked, actionable lists, so the first five minutes of a workday tell the owner exactly where to focus.

12Conversational assistantP1

Pain point

Even with a good dashboard, finding "who hasn't sent documents" or "prepare the filing for Client X" often means clicking through several screens.

Solution

A chat panel that answers questions from live firm data and opens the relevant page — "who is missing documents," "generate the VAT worksheet for Client X," "when is Client Y's next deadline."

How it works

The assistant only reads and summarises existing data, or triggers the same actions a person could click (schedule a reminder, open a page). It never sends a message or files anything without landing on the normal approval step. This keeps the "human always approves" principle intact even when AI is doing the asking.

13Optional client portalP1

Pain point

Some clients (or some firms) prefer a web upload link over WhatsApp, especially for larger files or for clients without WhatsApp.

Solution

A simple, no-login (magic-link) portal mirroring the same checklist, for firms or clients who want it, alongside WhatsApp rather than instead of it.

How it works

Same backend as the WhatsApp hub — the portal is just another way documents arrive. Uploaded files go through the identical capture → extract → review pipeline.

14Bank statement parsingP1

Pain point

Bank reconciliation is one of the most repetitive tasks in bookkeeping, and bank statement PDFs are notoriously inconsistent in format.

Solution

Parse bank statement PDFs/Excel into transaction lists and suggest matches against already-approved invoices.

How it works

Per-bank templates plus a general-purpose parser handle the most common statement formats first; suggested matches always require a human confirmation click, never an automatic post.

15Structured e-invoicing readinessP1

Pain point

A growing number of countries now require invoices as structured, government-validated data rather than PDFs. Getting a client's invoicing data clean enough for that transition is new, unpaid work for firms.

Solution

Check a client's invoice data against the structured-format field requirements, show a readiness score, and flag exactly what's missing (usually tax IDs, addresses or item-level detail).

How it works

Taxflow does not become a certified e-invoicing transmitter itself — that requires a formal accreditation process specific to each country. Instead, it prepares clean data and hands off to an accredited third-party provider via an integration, similar to how a firm might use a specialist e-filing partner today.

16Multi-jurisdiction rule packsP2

Pain point

An international firm (or a firm with clients abroad) juggles completely different filing calendars, forms and tax categories per country.

Solution

The deadline engine, worksheet builder and document-field rules are all driven by a swappable "country pack" rather than hard-coded logic, so adding a new jurisdiction doesn't mean rebuilding the product.

How it works

Each pack defines: filing types and calendars, required invoice fields and tax categories, worksheet/export mappings, and message templates in the local language. The core product (collect, extract, review, track) stays identical across every country.

17Team workload and capacity viewP2

Pain point

In a growing firm, work isn't evenly spread — one senior accountant quietly carries twice the client load of another.

Solution

Assign clients or filings to staff, and show a workload view alongside the deadline dashboard.

How it works

Built on the same task/step data already tracked for deadlines — this is a different view of existing data, not a new data model.

18Notifications and daily digestP2

Pain point

Staff shouldn't need to open the dashboard constantly to know something needs their attention.

Solution

A daily digest (email or in-app) summarising overnight activity: new documents received, items needing review, filings due soon.

How it works

A scheduled job compiles the same dashboard data into a digest at a time the firm chooses.

8. The trust and accuracy model

This is the section a skeptical accountant will read most carefully, so it gets its own space rather than being scattered across features.

Three trust tiers

  • Uncertain (AI-read): raw extraction from a document. Always shown with a confidence score and the source image. Never used downstream until approved.
  • Confirmed (human-approved): a person has reviewed and approved the field. This is the only data that feeds into worksheets, exports or client-facing summaries.
  • Deterministic (rule-calculated): totals, tax splits and box values, computed by fixed formulas from confirmed data — never estimated by a model.

Rules we don't break

  • No message is ever sent, and no filing is ever generated, without an explicit approval step (configurable to "notify me" or "require my click," never fully silent).
  • No number in a worksheet is unsourced — every figure is clickable back to the document it came from.
  • Accuracy is measured and published by document type and language, not asserted with one marketing number.
  • The product never claims to "file" anything. It prepares; the licensed firm files.
⚠️Getting this wrong is an existential risk, not a UX detail. A single wrongly-filed return traced back to an unreviewed AI extraction would be catastrophic for firm trust and for Taxflow's reputation. Every design decision in this document defers to this section when in conflict.

10. Core data model

Kept deliberately simple: everything hangs off Client and Document, because those are what an accountant actually thinks in.

Firm
 └─ Users (role: owner / senior / junior / read-only)
 └─ Client
     ├─ Contact (channel, language, consent status)
     ├─ Filing Obligation (type, frequency, jurisdiction rules)
     ├─ Checklist (per period) ── Request (message sent) ── Message (thread)
     ├─ Document (raw file, status: received → extracting → review → approved)
     │    └─ Extraction (fields, confidence per field, edit history)
     ├─ Task (a filing: steps, due date, status, assignee)
     └─ Output (worksheet or export file, linked to source Documents)
AuditEvent (actor, action, entity, before, after, timestamp)

Two design choices matter most here. First, Document and Extraction are separate records, so the original file and the AI's reading of it are never conflated — you can always see both. Second, Output always links back to the Documents behind it, so "where did this number come from" is a data relationship, not a manual lookup.

11. Architecture at a glance

Major components

  • Web application — the firm's workspace (dashboard, review queue, deadlines, worksheets).
  • Messaging service — integrates with the WhatsApp Business Platform via a licensed provider; handles templates, consent, inbound webhooks.
  • Ingestion pipeline — validates incoming files, deduplicates, queues for extraction.
  • Extraction service — OCR plus document-understanding model, with rule-based validation layered on top.
  • Rules engine — per-jurisdiction filing calendars, invoice field requirements, and worksheet/export mappings (the "country pack").
  • Vault — encrypted document storage, organised automatically by client/year/month/bucket.
  • Export/integration layer — format converters and API connectors to third-party ledgers.
  • Audit and billing services.

Key architectural decisions

  • Extraction and calculation are separate services. One is probabilistic (AI), the other deterministic (rules). Mixing them would make every number in the product only as trustworthy as the least reliable part.
  • Buy the commodity, build the workflow. Messaging infrastructure, OCR baselines and cloud hosting are bought. The firm-specific workflow — review queue, deadline engine, country packs — is what we build and defend.
  • Don't become a regulated party. Where a role requires formal accreditation (e.g. transmitting structured e-invoices to a government network), Taxflow integrates with an accredited partner rather than seeking that accreditation itself in the early stages.
  • Country packs, not forks. Expanding to a new market means adding a configuration pack, not maintaining a separate codebase.

15. Explicitly out of scope (v1)

  • Full general-ledger accounting (journal entries, trial balance, financial statement production) — this remains the job of the firm's existing accounting software.
  • Becoming an accredited e-invoicing transmitter or tax filing agent in any jurisdiction.
  • Submitting returns directly to any government portal on a firm's behalf.
  • Selling or marketing directly to end-client SMEs; they are users of the product, never the buyer.
  • Marketing-style bulk messaging to clients; all outbound messages are service-related to an existing engagement.
  • Payroll processing (may be considered as a later, clearly-scoped feature once the core loop is proven).
